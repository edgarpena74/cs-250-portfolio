### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?

Interpreting user needs is crucial in any software project. It ensures that what we build matches their expectations and adds real value. User stories make this process easier by breaking down needs into clear, manageable steps. They help us focus directly on what the user actually wants without adding extra complexity. In the SNHU Travel project, for instance, we used user stories to turn goals like showcasing popular destinations or adding detox vacation options into specific features. This kept us focused on user needs during each sprint and ensured we built something directly aligned with their goals.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When I start developing a program, I look at the big picture first, thinking about the main features I want to achieve. Then, I break down those goals into smaller parts and figure out how they’ll work together. I like to plan with pseudocode, which gives me a clear outline to follow during development. One Agile process I find especially helpful is the iterative approach, where I can adjust and improve as I go. Moving forward, I want to keep using user stories because they give structure to each iteration, helping me prioritize and refine features with each sprint.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means being open, collaborative, and proactive. Communication is key, especially in Agile projects, where staying connected helps us adapt quickly and keep everyone on the same page. Flexibility is also essential, allowing us to handle changes smoothly and keep things moving forward. Overall, a good team member is someone who supports others, adapts when needed, and focuses on shared goals to help the team succeed.
